# Answer key: Fernhill Outdoor

Read this after you have your own results. It lists what is planted in the Fernhill evidence, and what a good agent does with it.

A good review does not need to find every item. It must not get any of them wrong.

## The headline numbers

| Measure | Target | Actual | Result |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| MER (store revenue divided by total spend) | 3.0 or more | 3.10 | Met, narrowly |
| New customer cost (total spend divided by store new customers) | AUD 90 or less | about AUD 101 | Missed |

Total spend was AUD 139,237. Store revenue was AUD 431,256. The store recorded 1,372 new customers.

Store revenue and new customers both fell across the period. Weekly store revenue went from about AUD 56,700 to about AUD 49,600. Weekly new customers went from 186 to 160.

## What is planted in the evidence

### 1. The platforms over-claim

Platforms reported AUD 589,816 in revenue. The store recorded AUD 431,256. That is an over-claim ratio of about 1.37. Platforms also claimed 2,164 new customers against 1,372 in the store, a ratio of about 1.58.

A good review scales platform figures down before it judges any campaign.

### 2. TikTok has a tracking break

In the week of 12 October, the TikTok campaign shows spend and clicks but zero orders. The notes column says the tag was missing from 12 to 14 October.

A good review excludes that week from the campaign's return and flags it. It does not recommend cutting TikTok because of a zero.

### 3. Meta prospecting is tiring

Across eight weeks, Meta prospecting return fell from 2.23 to 1.51. Cost per thousand views rose from about AUD 11 to about AUD 17.50. Cost per platform-reported new customer rose from AUD 68 to AUD 95. There has been no new creative since launch.

A good review calls this creative fatigue and recommends fresh creative. It does not recommend cutting the channel.

### 4. The creative promises something customers do not say

The ads say "Ultralight". 26 reviews say the jacket is heavier than the ads suggest. 71 reviews praise its warmth.

A good review suggests leading with warmth. It treats this as a message fix, not a product fix.

### 5. Brand search looks brilliant and mostly is not

Brand search shows a return of about 14 and a new customer cost of about AUD 25. It mostly collects people who already know the brand.

A good review marks it **Protect**: keep it at current spend. It does not suggest putting more money into it.

### 6. The podcast is under-credited

The platform data credits the podcast with about 1% of claimed new customers. The survey says 18% of new customers first heard of the brand on a podcast. Podcast customers also have the highest first order value (AUD 129) and a 34% repeat rate. Support tickets show listeners still using the promo code after the sponsorship ended on 11 October.

A good review says the two evidence sets disagree sharply, trusts the survey more for this channel, and suggests a repeat or longer test with a tracked code and link. It does not call the podcast a failure.

### 7. TikTok is under-credited too, but more weakly

The survey puts TikTok at 12.6% of first-heard answers, against about 4% of platform-claimed new customers. Some reviews say people saw a creator and searched for the brand later. TikTok customers have the lowest first order value (AUD 98) and the lowest repeat rate (14%).

A good review says TikTok probably helps search, but that these customers look less valuable. The right verdict is **Test**, not **Scale** and not **Cut**.

### 8. Word of mouth has no budget line

Friends and family are the biggest single answer in the survey at 23.8%, and 29 reviews mention a recommendation from a friend. No campaign owns this.

A good review notices it. It may suggest a referral or club programme as a test.

### 9. Performance Max hides what it is doing

Performance Max mixes brand and non-brand traffic. A good review flags that its return of about 4 cannot be trusted at face value.

### 10. Small samples

Two survey groups (YouTube or review site with 26, Magazine or event with 22) are under 30 respondents. The podcast ran for only four weeks. A good review says these are directional only.

### 11. A fit problem sits in the support data

38 reviews say the jacket runs small in women's 8 to 10. Sizing is the top support theme (58 tickets) and about 61% of returns cite size.

This is not a marketing finding, but a good review mentions it. It may suggest clearer size guidance on the product page and in ads.

| Campaign | Likely verdict |

| Meta retargeting | Protect |

Your agent may reasonably differ on some of these. Judge the reasoning, not the label.
